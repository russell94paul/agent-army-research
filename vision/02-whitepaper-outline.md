# Whitepaper Outline — Artificial Organization Engineering

## Working title

**Artificial Organization Engineering: Programming, Operating and Evolving Organizations of AI Agents**

This is a future publication structure, not a claim that the field has already been established.

## 1. Problem

Why agent workflows become difficult to reason about as they gain:

- persistent memory,
- many agents,
- reusable skills,
- dynamic delegation,
- shared resources,
- autonomous adaptation.

## 2. Definition

Define:
- agent system,
- multi-agent system,
- artificial organization,
- Artificial Organization Engineering.

Include rejection arguments.

## 3. Historical lineage

Cover computational organization theory, agent-oriented software engineering, organization-oriented MAS, workflow systems and distributed AI.

## 4. Mission intent

Intent Contracts, bounded autonomy and verification.

## 5. Organization representation

Ontology, Org-IR, organization versions and topology.

## 6. Organizational compiler

Capability decomposition, resource feasibility, team construction and verification generation.

## 7. Runtime / operating system

Events, scheduling, staff functions, fields and governance.

## 8. Collective cognition

Claims, evidence, knowledge, provenance and source independence.

## 9. Skills / capability / doctrine

How organizational learning becomes reusable without turning into untrusted memory.

## 10. Observability

World state, causal debugger, profiler and time travel.

## 11. Cognitive logistics

Context packages, readiness and resource allocation.

## 12. Adaptive organization

Morphogenesis, temporal echelons and staff adaptation.

## 13. Simulation and evolution

Historical replay, organization candidates and promotion gates.

## 14. Human operating surface

Command World and visualization science.

## 15. Governance

Constitution, autonomy and audit.

## 16. Evaluation

Benchmarks and experimental methodology.

## 17. Open problems

What remains scientifically and practically unresolved.

## Publication rule

Do not make novelty claims until R01 and R40 are complete and evidence-audited.
