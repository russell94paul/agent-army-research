# Draft Laws of Artificial Organizations

These are hypotheses for research, not established truths.

## 1. Intent Before Topology

Do not choose a team structure before the desired outcome and constraints are explicit.

## 2. Evidence Before Autonomy

The stronger an organization's authority, the stronger its verification requirements should be.

## 3. Knowledge Without Provenance Is Rumor

Repeated claims derived from a single source do not become independent evidence.

## 4. Context Is Logistics

Context, models, tools, permissions and attention are scarce supplies that should be deliberately allocated.

## 5. Capability Is Earned

Capability should derive from demonstrated outcomes, not role labels.

## 6. Local Autonomy Requires Global Boundaries

Agents can choose local actions only inside explicit authority, budget and safety envelopes.

## 7. Organizational State Must Be Observable Before It Is Self-Modifying

Do not permit adaptive topology or policy mutation that cannot be reconstructed and explained.

## 8. Doctrine Changes Slower Than Strategy

A persistent organizational rule requires more evidence than a one-mission tactical decision.

## 9. Simulation Precedes Evolution

Self-optimization should first occur in replay, simulation and shadow environments.

## 10. Activity Is Not Progress

Measure verified state change and mission outcomes, not message count or tool calls.

## 11. Shared Understanding Is a Runtime Asset

Agents should access relevant common state without requiring repeated conversational synchronization.

## 12. Organizational Learning Must Have a Promotion Path

Experience is not automatically knowledge. Knowledge is not automatically skill. Skill is not automatically doctrine.

## 13. Organizational Structure Has a Cost

Every new agent, role, communication edge and review layer consumes coordination resources.

## 14. An Organization Should Know What It Does Not Know

Uncertainty and coverage must be represented explicitly.

## 15. Humans Own Constitutions

The artificial organization may optimize execution but cannot silently redefine hard governance boundaries.
