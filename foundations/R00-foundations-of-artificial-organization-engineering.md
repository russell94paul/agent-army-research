# R00 — Foundations of Artificial Organization Engineering

## Status

FOUNDATIONAL RESEARCH. Run before most specialized prompts.

## Role

Act as:

- a principal multi-agent systems researcher,
- computational organization theorist,
- distributed-systems architect,
- programming-languages researcher,
- organizational-science researcher,
- developer-tools architect,
- HCI researcher,
- AI evaluation researcher,
- and skeptical historian of computing.

Your job is to determine whether **Artificial Organization Engineering (AOE)** is a coherent engineering discipline or merely a new label applied to existing multi-agent systems.

Do not protect the thesis.

Try to falsify it.

---

# 1. Working hypothesis

An artificial organization is not merely a collection of agents.

It may be a structured, persistent, executable system containing:

```text
intent
missions
roles
agents
teams
capabilities
skills
knowledge
evidence
resources
policies
communication structures
authority
history
evaluation
learning
organizational topology
```

The organization may:

- instantiate workers,
- allocate resources,
- maintain knowledge,
- delegate authority,
- adapt topology,
- verify outcomes,
- learn procedures,
- preserve institutional memory,
- and evolve across missions.

Research whether that set of concerns deserves a first-class engineering discipline.

# 8. Proposed laws

Research whether AOE can derive useful design laws.

Candidate laws to attack:

```text
Intent before topology.
Evidence before autonomy.
Knowledge without provenance is rumor.
Local autonomy requires global constraints.
Context is a resource, not a transcript.
Organizations should expose state before changing state.
Doctrine should change slower than strategy.
Simulation should precede self-modifying optimization.
Capability should be measured from outcomes, not role names.
Human-controlled constitutions bound machine-controlled execution.
```

Do not simply polish these.

Find counterexamples.

Create better laws.
